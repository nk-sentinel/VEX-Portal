Why Tier 3 has no toggle

Tier 3 evidence — CVSS vector, EPSS, KEV, exposure — may raise severity or route a finding to a human. It may never clear one. That is enforced in the domain layer, not by reviewer discipline.

A greyed-out toggle would imply the capability exists and is merely switched off, so the control is absent instead.

Design notes

Where this came from, and what the spec left open

Read this before the screens.

Design context

Nothing here is invented. The visual system is DAST-Portal's, lifted from web/src/app/core/design/ — the Signal palette, IBM Plex Sans and Mono, the dark rail that stays dark in both themes, the one table style, the Operative control vocabulary. VEX Portal mirrors DAST-Portal so the same rotation maintains both, and that has to hold for the interface too.

What is new is only what VEX needs and DAST has no concept of: an outcome ramp, an evidence-tier ramp, a deliberately colourless escalation-signal block, an assessment-state ramp and an expiry treatment. Those live in styles/tokens.scss under a VEX-SPECIFIC heading, and in outcome.scss and evidence-drawer.scss.

Three rules the CSS enforces, not just the copy

Risk Acceptance Required is never styled as a determination. It is the only outcome that is not a filled pill: dashed violet outline, an outbound arrow on its actions, a dashed row edge. Nothing about it borrows the green of a cleared finding.

Escalation signals carry no colour at all. EPSS 0.944 and a KEV listing sit in muted grey on a recessed surface with a permanent “not a basis for clearing” line. There is no red-EPSS token to reach for, so no future change can accidentally place a severity colour next to a Not Affected verdict.

A cleared verdict always shows its tier. The tier badge is a sibling of the outcome pill everywhere the outcome appears — queue, detail, drawer, requester result.

The three open questions, answered

1. Queue grouping. Both, remembered. Finding-level is the default because that is the working mode; the choice persists per user, so a first-time reviewer starts flat and a tracker who switches stays switched.

2. Requester and the rule trace. Plain-language reason first, full trace behind a per-finding disclosure. The requester who wants to learn can open it; the requester who wants the answer is not made to read a trace to find it.

3. Risk Acceptance in the queue. In both, visually set apart. Hiding it from the queue would make the reviewer's count disagree with reality; the dashed treatment carries the distinction instead.

Gaps in the spec, and what I did about them

Tier 2 second confirmation had no design. The spec requires the UI to show that one is needed and by whom, but not how. It is a dashed strip inside the determination block, in the tier 2 blue, naming the confirmer once given and stating plainly that you cannot provide both.

Bulk refusal had no surface. “Accept recommendations must refuse a selection and say which rows blocked it” — so the refusal is a red callout above the table listing every blocking CVE with its reason, and nothing is partially applied.

Keyboard model had no discoverability. A queue that is fully keyboard operable and never says so is keyboard operable for the person who wrote it. There is a persistent hint strip above the table and a ? panel.

Expiry was described but never drawn. Three bands: normal, within 48 hours, lapsed — each with its own token, used identically on the assessment list and the dashboard.

Abstention had no shape. Where the adjudicator abstained, the drawer lists what evidence was missing, as its own block, distinct from a failed collector. Those two look nothing alike on purpose: one is a limit of the evidence, the other is an outage.

No persona switch existed, and a mock needs one. The rail carries a persona select. It is a mockup affordance, not a product feature — in the real portal the role comes from AD group membership.

Threshold impact preview. Screen 9 requires showing how many of the last 30 days' findings would have routed differently before saving. Type a new EPSS value and the callout recomputes.

Still open

· Below 1100px the drawer goes full-screen. The back affordance is the close button; a dedicated back arrow may read better on a tablet.

· Dashboard drill-through is drawn but not wired — every number is meant to link to its rows.

· Notifications are absent. DAST has a feed; VEX's spec does not mention one, and expiry warnings are the obvious first candidate.

· Sample data is invented. Real CVE and component names, fabricated evidence.
